Rebind

Don’t rip books. Knit and wear them.

To learn from a book, an AI cuts it into pieces and keeps no note of where each piece came from. Rebind has an AI read a book and hands back a scarf pattern in which every row leads to the sentences it was made from.

Scan to see the scarves knitted on screen, click any row, and print the pattern.

    The question. Can a model’s reading of a whole book be shown so that anyone can trace what they see, by hand, back to the sentences behind it? And how steady is that reading?

    What comes next. People label a sample blind, to learn if the labels are right and not just steady. Readers try to find a scene from the scarf alone. Someone knits one. A closed-book mode gives a pattern without the text, for books still in copyright.

    Why a scarf

    In 2025 a court described how Anthropic built the library it trained its AI from. It bought millions of printed books, cut off their bindings, scanned the pages and threw the paper away. The judge ruled that lawful. Millions more books had come from pirate libraries, and for those the company settled with authors for $1.5 billion in 2026.

    Lawful or not, the picture stayed with me: books taken apart so a machine could learn from them, with nothing left to say which page taught it what. I wanted to try the opposite. Let an AI read a book, keep the book whole, and hand back something a person can hold and check row by row.

    From 2018 until Covid in 2020 I taught children to knit. So when I looked for a way to keep a book, I reached for yarn.

    From the knitting classes, 2018 to 2020. Children’s faces are covered.

    How a book becomes a scarf

    1. A model reads it. A model running on a laptop reads a passage at a time, without being told which book it is. It labels every sentence warm, cold or neither, and tense or calm, and says who is there, pointing at the sentence that shows it.
    2. Code checks and counts. A claim is kept only if the sentence it points at exists and says so. Then the labels are counted by the rules below. Nothing else decides a stitch.
    3. The count is a pattern. One passage is one pair of rows, written as instructions a knitter can follow. Every row names its passage, so the scarf unravels back to the page.

    The rules, in full

      Builds on Rudin 2019, models a person can follow over explained black boxes · Jacovi & Goldberg 2020, faithful explanations · Sclar et al. 2024, wording moves results · Chang et al. 2023, books a model has memorised · Reagan et al. 2016, emotional arcs · Jansen et al. 2015, data you can hold. Full references in the repository.

      How far to trust it
